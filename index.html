Signal concepts

Frequency, period, amplitude, phase

Frequency f is cycles per second (Hz). Period T is seconds per cycle. Amplitude A is the peak size. Phase φ is where in its cycle the wave starts.

T = 1 / f     x(t) = A · sin(2πft + φ)

t is time in seconds. At 100 Hz, T is 10 ms.

Waveform shapes

Sine: a single frequency; the building block of the others. Square: odd harmonics at 1/n strength; digital clocks, chiptunes. Triangle: odd harmonics at 1/n²; softer, used in synthesisers. Sawtooth: all harmonics at 1/n; bright synth tones, timing ramps.

The display draws ideal shapes. Audio is band-limited, so sharp edges have slight ripples.

Interference and beats

Signals add point by point. In phase they reinforce; 180° apart with equal frequency and amplitude they cancel. Two close frequencies drift in and out of step:

fbeat = |f₁ − f₂|

Sampling and aliasing

Sampling measures a signal fs times per second. To capture it unambiguously, fs must exceed twice its highest frequency. Otherwise it appears as a lower alias:

falias = |f − fs · round(f / fs)|
